So there's tension between: - **Credibility:** If a nuclear attack goes unanswered, deterrence collapses globally and invites future attacks. - **Morality and consequences:** Retaliation that targets cities is hard to justify under just war principles (discrimination between combatants and civilians, proportionality), and it risks escalation. Most serious thinking lands on "a response is necessary, but its form matters enormously." The real debate is between nuclear and conventional responses, and between counterforce (military targets) and countervalue (cities). ## If it were China This is the hardest case, because China can strike back. - **The case for nuclear retaliation:** Failing to respond to a peer nuclear power could signal that the US won't defend itself, inviting further strikes and unraveling alliances like those with Japan and South
