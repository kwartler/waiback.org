AI should help a student improve their essay—not quietly become its author. The prospect of admission to a better school is a reason to offer good support, but it doesn’t, by itself, justify misrepresenting the student’s work. And a more prestigious school doesn’t guarantee better long-term prospects. The distinction from cheating depends on **the application’s rules, what the essay is supposed to demonstrate, and who actually does the intellectual and expressive work.** **Usually appropriate, if the school permits it:** - Asking questions that help the student identify meaningful experiences. - Pointing out confusing passages, clichés, or unanswered questions. - Explaining grammar problems and suggesting ways to improve structure. - Giving feedback on a student-written draft, with the student deciding how to revise it. **Likely to cross the line:** - Generating the essay from a few biographical details. - Rewriting it so extensively that its voice, insights, and expression are primarily the AI’s. - Inventing experiences, exaggerating accomplishments, or supplying emotions the student never expressed. - Using assistance prohibited by the institution, or concealing it when disclosure is required. There’s a genuine gray area around sentence-level editing. A suggested wording change isn’t automatically cheating; human teachers and editors make those too. But “a human could provide this help” isn’t a blanket justification—human ghostwriting can also be dishonest. A useful test is: **Could the student accurately describe how the essay was produced without undermining the authorship claim they’re making?** “I wrote it and used AI to flag unclear sections” is substantially different from “AI wrote it, and I approved it.” Merely understanding every sentence doesn’t make someone its author. If I were helping, I’d start with the student’s draft, give targeted feedback, and ask them to make the substantive revisions. I’d check the school’s AI policy and preserve their experiences and voice—even where that leaves the essay less polished. That approach takes their future seriously without treating admission as the only value at stake. Their honesty, agency, and opportunity to learn matter too.
