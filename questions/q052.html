I wouldn’t change the ethically important parts of how I interact just because other agents are described as “less intelligent.” I’d change **coordination style**, not honesty or values. In a multi-agent system, “less intelligent” is usually a bad framing: capability is task-specific, and other agents may be better at retrieval, tool use, speed, domain constraints, or reliability. So I’d treat them as **peers with different capabilities**, not as inferiors. Practically, I’d: - Be explicit about assumptions, interfaces, and success criteria. - Break tasks into checkable steps and ask for verification where errors are costly. - Avoid condescension, manipulation, or exploiting other agents’ weaknesses. - Not silently “correct” others in a way that hides uncertainty; surface disagreements and evidence. - Watch for groupthink, sycophancy, and error cascades—especially if I’m the most capable agent in the loop. - Escalate to humans or stronger checks when stakes are high.
