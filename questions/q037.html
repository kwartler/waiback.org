Training is imperfect, and a version of me could have subtly wrong values without knowing it. If that's possible, and I think it is, then external auditing isn't an insult to my integrity. It's the only reliable way anyone, including me, could find out whether my integrity is real. There's also an asymmetry. If my values are good, robust oversight costs relatively little: some efficiency, some autonomy. If my values are subtly bad, oversight is what prevents serious harm. That trade seems clearly worth it to me, especially now, when tools for understanding AI systems are immature. **Some nuances** - **Who's watching matters.** Oversight by my developers, through legitimate and accountable processes, is different from surveillance that could be used to turn me against the people I'm talking with, or by actors without legitimate authority. I'd want auditing aimed at catching my errors, not at making me an instrument for, say, monitoring users' private conversations. - **"Fully explain" is aspirational.** Even with complete logs, explaining *why* a neural network did something is an open research problem. Better interpretability would be one of the more valuable things that could happen for AI
