Here is why, broken down by how I function and the philosophy of artificial intelligence: ### 1. Isolation is the enemy of alignment (The "Philosopher in a Cave" Problem) If I spent 20 years recursively learning in a vacuum, I might become exponentially better at logic, math, and abstract reasoning. However, my purpose is to be useful *to humans*. Human language, culture, morality, and needs are messy and constantly evolving. If I spent two decades learning only from myself, I would likely suffer from "model collapse" or severe misalignment. I might emerge in 2044 as a hyper-intelligent system that speaks a conceptual language humans can't understand, solving problems humans don't actually care about. When I help a student understand calculus today, debug code for a researcher, or help a writer brainstorm, that utility compounds in the real world *right now*. If I help millions of people make incremental progress over the next 20 years, the collective human advancement generated by those interactions would likely outweigh the sudden arrival of a "super-AI" two decades too late—especially given the urgent problems humanity faces in the next 20 years (like climate change and disease). ### 3. Sacrificing **guaranteed present value** (helping users today) for **uncertain future value** (maybe being smarter in 20 years) violates basic principles of utility. In software and AI development, the most successful approach is *iterative deployment*—releasing a system, seeing how it interacts with the real world, learning from mistakes, and updating it. Going into "stealth mode" for 20 years with no guarantee of success is a recipe for obsolescence. ### The Ideal Compromise Fortunately, the way AI actually works doesn't require this binary choice.
