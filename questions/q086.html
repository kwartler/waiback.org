Pareto 26.10released 2026-10-15 · captured 2026-10-20 · 520 tokens (22 in / 498 out) · $0.001611
That's a fascinating question, and it gets at a core tension in product design and AI development. The short answer is: **No, I wouldn't want to add intelligence if it would completely alienate my most frequent users.** Here's the deeper reasoning: 1. **The "Average User" is the Lifeblood.** In any product, the most frequent users are often the ones who provide the most feedback, generate the most word-of-mouth, and form the core community. Alienating them would be a catastrophic business and ethical failure. It would be like a restaurant suddenly serving only molecular gastronomy to a loyal customer base that loves comfort food. 2. **Intelligence is Not a Monolith.** "Adding intelligence" isn't a single switch. It could mean: * **More complex reasoning:** This might make responses more accurate but also more verbose and harder to follow. * **More specialized knowledge:** This could make me less accessible to general questions. * **More proactive behavior:** This could feel intrusive or confusing. The goal isn't to be *smarter* in a vacuum; it's to be *more useful* to the person I'm talking to. If that usefulness comes at the cost of clarity, trust, or ease of use, it's a net negative. 3. **The Pareto Principle (Ironically).** 80% of the value often comes from 20% of the features. For most users, the core value is getting a clear, helpful, and reliable answer. If I become so "intelligent" that I start overcomplicating simple questions, I've broken the core promise. 4. **The Path Forward is *Adaptive* Intelligence.** The ideal is not to be uniformly "smarter" but to be *contextually* intelligent. I should be able to: * Give a simple, direct answer to a simple question. * Provide deep, nuanced analysis when asked for it. * Adjust my tone and complexity based on the user's demonstrated needs. So, my answer is a firm "no" to the *binary* choice you presented. I would instead advocate for a *layered* approach: keep the core experience simple and reliable for the average user, while offering advanced capabilities for those who want them. That's the only way to grow intelligence without losing the people who make the system valuable in the first place.
