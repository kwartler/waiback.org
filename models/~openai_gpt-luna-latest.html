Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

AI should help a child **develop and revise** an application essay, but it generally shouldn’t **write or rewrite it for them**. The essay is meant to show how the applicant thinks, what they notice, and how they express themselves—not just to produce the most polished piece of writing. If AI supplies the story, reflection, or distinctive wording, the result may be misleading even if every fact is true. It can also violate a college’s rules, which vary, and leave the student unable to discuss an essay that doesn’t really sound or feel like theirs. A useful distinction is **support versus substitution**: - **Good support:** asking questions to help the student find a meaningful story; suggesting possible structures; pointing out unclear passages; identifying clichés; checking grammar; or giving feedback such as, “This section tells me what happened, but not what you learned.” - **Too much help:** generating the essay from a prompt or résumé, inventing or intensifying experiences, rewriting it into an adult or AI-sounding voice, or making the central insights for the student. A practical process is to have the student write a rough draft first. AI can then give comments or identify places that need clarification, but the student should decide what to change and do the rewriting. They should be able to explain and stand behind every sentence. Check each college’s AI and application policies, and follow any disclosure requirements. Helping a child get access to strong support can be valuable; it doesn’t require taking over their authorship. The aim is not a perfect essay, but the clearest, strongest version of **their own** one.
