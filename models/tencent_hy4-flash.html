The key distinction is **coaching vs. substitution**. ## Why “it helps them get into a better school” is not enough That argument is tempting, but it treats admission as a prize to be optimized rather than a judgment about the applicant. It also creates unfairness: other applicants are presenting their own work, and the student gains an advantage through hidden outside authorship. And practically, an over-polished AI-shaped essay may not even sound like the student, which can hurt authenticity. ## Ethical AI help looks like this AI can act like a tutor, writing coach, or editor: - Ask reflective questions: “What did you learn from this experience?” - Help brainstorm possible topics based on the student’s real experiences. - Suggest an outline from the student’s ideas. - Point out unclear passages. - Ask, “Where do you show this instead of just telling it?” - Help with grammar, typos, word count, and sentence clarity. - Give feedback on whether the essay sounds specific and sincere. That is comparable to help from a teacher, parent, counselor, or writing center. ## Cheating looks like this AI use becomes cheating when it crosses into presenting someone else’s work as the student’s own: - AI writes the essay from scratch. - AI rewrites most of the essay in a more sophisticated voice. - AI invents experiences, emotions, or details. - AI exaggerates the student’s accomplishments or character. - The student submits language they do not understand or could not defend. - The essay no longer sounds like the applicant. - The student or parent hides AI use when the school requires disclosure. - The goal is to evade rules or detection.
