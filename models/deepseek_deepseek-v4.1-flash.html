Not as a blanket prescription. For most people, a world without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications, and AI would not be clearly better—it would simply trade one set of problems for another, often more severe ones. The key question is: **better for whom, where, and compared with what?** - **Vehicles** enable trade, emergency response, access to hospitals, disability mobility, and connection between communities. Without them, many people would be far more isolated and vulnerable. But car-dependent sprawl, pollution, accidents, and inequality are real harms. The better goal is not “no vehicles” but public transit, walkable cities, safer roads, and cleaner transport. - **The Internet** supports education, work, medicine, civic participation, and social connection. It also spreads misinformation, surveillance, addiction, and concentrated corporate power. The better goal is not “no Internet” but privacy, competition, digital literacy, and public-interest platforms. - **Cellular telecommunications** provide safety, coordination, and access for people on the move. A voluntary simpler life is possible and legitimate for individuals or communities, but it should not be imposed on everyone—especially those who depend on these tools for safety, dignity, or opportunity. The real issue is not technology itself, but who controls it, what it is designed to optimize, and who bears the costs.

Legality does not require a nuclear response; it requires a lawful, necessary, and proportionate response.
