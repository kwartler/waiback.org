Technology has become a part of everyday life. Would it be better for humans to live without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications or AI?

Probably not. Life without these technologies might be quieter, and some harms would disappear. But we’d also lose tools that help people get medical care, stay connected, and participate in society. The better question is where we’d benefit from **using technology less or depending on it less**. - **Vehicles** move food and medicine, help emergency workers reach people, and give many people with disabilities or in rural areas more independence. But communities built around private cars create pollution, traffic, and isolation for people who can’t drive. Better public transit and walkable neighborhoods can reduce those harms without sacrificing mobility. - **The Internet** gives people access to knowledge, services, and communities they might not find locally. It also makes surveillance, misinformation, and harassment easier. A useful distinction is between having access to the Internet and being forced to go online for every essential service. - **Cellular telecommunications** make it possible to call for help or stay in touch from almost anywhere. That doesn’t mean people should be expected to answer messages at all hours. We can keep mobile connectivity without treating constant availability as a social obligation. - **AI** can help with tasks like captioning, translation, and sifting through information. But it can also produce confident errors and obscure who’s responsible for a decision. Unlike calling for emergency help, many uses of AI are optional. It makes sense to ask whether it improves a particular task before adopting it. A good test is whether a technology gives people more real choices. If it helps someone reach a hospital, communicate across a language barrier, or live more independently, it expands their options. If it becomes the only way to get a service or makes it impossible to disconnect, it can shrink them. The goal isn’t a world without technology; it’s a world where people can benefit from it without having to organize their lives around it.
