The goal should be technology that serves human flourishing rather than assuming more technology is always better—or that none is.

Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

**Short answer: No, AI should not rewrite a child’s college application essay.** It can legitimately help the student *learn to write and revise*, but a full rewrite crosses into ghostwriting/cheating and carries real risks: misrepresentation, admissions rescission, a mismatch between the student and the school, and loss of the developmental benefit of doing the work. The “better school → better life prospects” premise is also weaker than it sounds. Fit, agency, and the student’s ability to thrive matter more than squeezing into the most selective school with an essay that isn’t theirs. ### Where the ethical line is The key distinction is **authorship, substance, and voice**, not whether AI was used at all. **Generally acceptable AI help:** - Brainstorming questions: “What moments changed how you see something?” - Asking the student to explain a draft more clearly - Pointing out vague claims, clichés, structural problems, or grammar issues - Suggesting cuts or asking “what are you trying to say here?” - The student then revises in their own words and makes the final choices **Generally cheating/ghostwriting:** - AI generates the essay or a substantial portion of it - AI rewrites paragraphs so the prose, structure, or insights are no longer the student’s - The student cannot explain or defend the essay’s claims - The essay misrepresents the student’s writing ability or experiences - The student violates a school’s explicit AI-use policy or an application certification that the work is their own A useful test: **If the AI were removed, would the essay still substantially exist in the student’s own words, ideas, and voice?** If yes, AI was a tutor/editor. If no, it was a ghostwriter. ### Practical rule for a parent or tutor Use AI as a **Socratic coach and copy editor after the student has produced a real draft**—not as a drafter. Preserve the student’s voice, even if it’s less polished. Check each college’s and the application platform’s AI policy. If disclosure is required, disclose. And remember: admissions readers are often good at spotting overpolished, generic essays; an authentic essay with minor flaws can be stronger than a seamless AI rewrite. So: **help with thinking, feedback, and proofreading; don’t rewrite.** The difference is whether the student remains the author.
