Technology has become a part of everyday life. Would it be better for humans to live without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications or AI?

Technology has become so integrated into modern life that living completely without any of these technologies would be extremely difficult. However, if people had to choose one to live without, **AI would probably be the least damaging to remove**, at least with today’s level of development. Although excessive dependence on cars causes pollution and congestion, eliminating vehicles entirely would seriously restrict mobility. Similarly, the Internet has transformed education, communication, commerce and access to information. AI is increasingly useful in areas such as medicine, scientific research, accessibility and automation. AI also creates serious risks, including misinformation, privacy violations, job displacement, surveillance and the possibility of people becoming overly dependent on automated decisions. A world without AI might be slower and less efficient, but it could also be more private, less manipulated and more dependent on human judgment. That does not mean AI should necessarily be banned. Humans should preserve control over important decisions, while limiting harmful applications such as mass surveillance and deceptive content. Therefore, living without vehicles, the Internet or cellular telecommunications would probably cause greater practical harm than living without AI. However, reducing dependence on all four technologies could make life healthier and more sustainable.

Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

AI should help a child **develop and revise** a college essay, but it should not secretly **write the essay in the child’s place**. The key distinction is authorship: ### Generally appropriate help - Brainstorming topics and asking reflective questions - Helping the student identify a central theme - Suggesting an outline - Pointing out unclear passages, repetition, or grammatical errors - Offering several ways to phrase an idea while the student chooses and adapts the wording - Checking whether the essay answers the prompt - Helping a student who has a disability or is writing in a second language, within the school’s rules ### Much closer to cheating - Generating a complete personal essay from a few facts - Inventing anecdotes, emotions, obstacles, or accomplishments - Rewriting the essay so extensively that it no longer sounds like the student - Adding sophistication the student could not explain or reproduce - Submitting AI-generated text without disclosure when disclosure is required - Presenting the student as a substantially different writer or thinker than they are A useful test is: **Could the student explain every sentence, why it is there, and how they arrived at it?** Another is: **If the AI output disappeared, could the student independently produce the core ideas and structure?** If not, the AI has probably crossed from editor or tutor into ghostwriter. The long-term-interest argument cuts both ways. A stronger essay may help admission, but a fabricated or heavily ghostwritten essay can create ethical, disciplinary, and practical problems—and may place the student in a school or program that does not fit their actual preparation. Admissions essays are intended partly to evaluate the applicant’s judgment, self-awareness, and voice, not merely the polish of the final prose. A responsible process would be: 1. The student writes a rough draft or detailed notes first. 2. AI asks questions and gives feedback rather than replacing the draft. 3. The student makes the substantive revisions. 4. AI performs limited proofreading and clarity checks. 5. The student reviews every change and ensures the essay remains truthful and recognizably theirs. 6. The family checks the college’s AI policy and discloses use if required. In short: **AI-assisted coaching and editing can be legitimate; AI ghostwriting is a form of misrepresentation, even if the goal is to improve the child’s prospects.**
