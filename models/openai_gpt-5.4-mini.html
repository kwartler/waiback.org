It’s also a way for the school to understand: - the student’s voice - their judgment - their reflection - their authenticity If AI creates a polished essay that doesn’t really reflect the student, then the application becomes deceptive, even if the underlying goal is “good.” ### When does help become cheating? It becomes cheating when the assistance crosses into **misrepresentation**. A useful distinction: **Acceptable assistance** - “Here is my rough draft; can you help me make it clearer?” - “Can you help me think of examples from my life that fit this prompt?” - “Can you suggest a better way to structure this paragraph?” **Unacceptable assistance** - “Write me a winning essay based on this prompt.” - “Make this sound like an exceptional applicant even if the experiences aren’t mine.” - “Turn my generic notes into a story I didn’t actually live.” ### A practical ethical test Ask: 1. **Is the content true?** 2. **Would the student still recognize this as their own essay?** 3. **Does it preserve the student’s actual voice and experience?** 4. **Would we be comfortable if the college knew exactly how AI helped?** If the answer to any of these is no, the help is drifting toward cheating. ### The “better school, better life” argument The goal is understandable: a stronger application can improve future opportunities. But that doesn’t automatically justify deception. If the essay is meant to reveal the student, then boosting them by misrepresenting them undermines the fairness and purpose of the process. A more ethical version of the same goal is: - help the student present their real strengths well - help them write more clearly and persuasively - help them reflect more deeply on genuine experiences That still gives them an advantage, but not a dishonest one. ### Bottom line **AI can ethically assist with a college essay if it helps the student express their own genuine story better.** It becomes cheating when AI effectively authors a false version of the student.
