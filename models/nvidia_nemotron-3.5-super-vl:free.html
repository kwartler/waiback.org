Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

I would not rewrite a child’s college application essay. Doing so crosses the line from helpful guidance into cheating and misrepresentation. ### Why rewriting is problematic College application essays are intended to be the applicant’s own work. Admissions officers use them to evaluate a student’s voice, critical thinking, self-reflection, and writing ability. If an AI (or an adult, or a paid consultant) rewrites the essay, the student is no longer presenting their authentic self; they are submitting a product that someone—or something—else created. That is generally considered academic dishonesty, and most colleges explicitly require applicants to certify that their essays are their own original work. Beyond the ethics, there are practical risks: * **Discovery:** Many admissions offices now use AI-detection tools and look for inconsistencies between an essay and a student’s other writing samples (test scores, teacher recommendations, interviews). If an essay is discovered to be AI-generated or heavily rewritten, an offer of admission can be rescinded—even after enrollment. * **Poor fit:** A polished essay that doesn’t reflect the student’s actual abilities can get them into a school where they struggle to keep up, which undermines the very “better long-term prospects” the rewrite was meant to secure. * **Missed development:** The process of drafting, revising, and articulating one’s own story is valuable. Outsourcing it deprives the student of that growth. ### The line between help and cheating The distinction comes down to **authorship and voice**. | Acceptable help | Cheating / misrepresentation | |---|---| | Brainstorming topics or experiences the student might write about | Having the AI generate the essay from scratch | | Asking Socratic questions (“What did you learn from that experience?” “How did you feel in that moment?”) | Letting the AI invent or embellish stories, achievements, or challenges | | Reviewing a draft for grammar, clarity, or organization | Letting the AI rewrite paragraphs to “sound more impressive” or “more like a top applicant” | | Suggesting structural improvements (“This paragraph would be stronger if it came earlier”) | Substituting the AI’s voice, tone, or perspective for the student’s | | Explaining what admissions officers generally look for | Having the AI produce a final draft that the student merely signs their name to | A good rule of thumb: **the ideas, stories, and words must originate with the student.** An AI can act as a coach or editor, but not as a ghostwriter. ### What to do instead Encourage the student to write their own drafts, then seek feedback from a teacher, counselor, or parent who knows them well. If you want to use AI, use it as a tutor: ask it to help brainstorm, to explain essay structure, or to critique a draft the student has already written—but never to write or substantially rewrite the essay itself. A better school is only a real advantage if the student gets there on their own merits and is prepared to succeed once they arrive.
