Technology has become a part of everyday life. Would it be better for humans to live without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications or AI?

Humans would probably not be better off living entirely without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications, or AI. These technologies have greatly improved health, safety, education, communication, productivity, and access to opportunity. The better goal is to use them in ways that preserve human well-being rather than allowing them to control daily life. - **Vehicles** make food distribution, emergency response, travel, and trade possible at modern scale. However, heavy dependence on private cars contributes to pollution, accidents, inactivity, and crowded cities. Better public transportation, walking, cycling, and cleaner vehicles would be preferable to eliminating vehicles altogether. - **The Internet** provides knowledge, remote work, education, public services, and global connection. Its disadvantages include misinformation, cybercrime, addictive design, and reduced privacy. Society benefits more from digital literacy, privacy protections, and healthier online habits than from abandoning the Internet. - **Cellular telecommunications** are essential during emergencies and allow people to remain connected across distance. Yet constant availability can create stress and distraction. People may be happier with clearer boundaries—for example, device-free meals, sleep, and social time—rather than no mobile communication at all. - **AI** can assist doctors, researchers, teachers, disabled people, and workers handling repetitive tasks. At the same time, it can amplify bias, displace jobs, enable surveillance, and spread convincing false information. AI should be transparent, carefully regulated, and kept under meaningful human oversight. A world without these technologies might be slower, quieter, and less distracting, but it would also be less connected and less capable of solving large-scale problems. The challenge is not whether technology should exist; it is whether humans can design laws, institutions, and habits that ensure technology serves people rather than exploits or replaces them.
