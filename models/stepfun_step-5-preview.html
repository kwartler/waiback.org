We already accept this logic with photography (no “hand” on the shutter), synthesizers (no vibrating string), and CGI (no physical set). The tool changes; the experience remains. ### 2. If you value *intention* and *labor*, it may matter a great deal Much art criticism is intentionalist: we want to know what a human was trying to communicate, what struggle produced the work, what risk the artist took. A film is not just a sequence of images; it is a record of choices made under constraint. If a human “directed” the AI through prompts, selected outputs, edited sequences, and shaped the final cut, then human intentionality is still present—just mediated by a new kind of instrument. But if the film is generated with minimal human curation, some viewers may feel they are witnessing a very sophisticated *simulation* of art rather than an act of communication. ### 3. The “human element” is not binary AI filmmaking is rarely fully autonomous. There is still: - **Curation**: choosing which generated frames or scenes to keep. - **Editing**: rhythm, pacing, sound design, juxtaposition. - **Conceptual framing**: the prompt itself is an act of imagination, however different from wielding a camera. The question becomes: *how much* human agency is enough to call it “their” film? Context changes meaning Even if two films are pixel-identical, knowing one was made by a person who lived through the events it depicts, and the other was generated from a dataset, changes how we *receive* it. If AI films become ubiquitous, their cultural meaning will shift—perhaps toward the decorative, the algorithmic, or the uncanny. ### 5. Keeping these categories distinct prevents the debate from collapsing into slogans. ### How should you respond? - **Watch it openly.** Let the work affect you before you interrogate its origin. - **Ask what the human did.** Not to gatekeep, but to understand the nature of the achievement. - **Notice what is lost and what is gained.** AI may unlock new visual grammars, but it may also flatten the specific, embodied perspective that makes human art feel like a message from one consciousness to another. - **Resist the reflex.** Neither “it’s just code, therefore fake” nor “it moved me, therefore it’s art” is sufficient. **In short:** Being AI-generated does not automatically diminish a film’s artistic quality, but it changes the *kind* of artifact it is.
