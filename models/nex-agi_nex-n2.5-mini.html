No—if by “rewrite” you mean replacing the student’s draft with a polished, admissions-optimized version, I would not do that. That crosses from support into ghostwriting, even if the goal is beneficial. A better distinction is: ## AI should help the student think and communicate, not author the essay. Acceptable help: - Ask questions that help the student clarify their story. - Give feedback on clarity, structure, tone, and specificity. - Help identify repetitive or vague passages. - Suggest grammar and sentence-level improvements. - Help the student turn rough notes into an outline. - Explain what admissions readers might be looking for. Unacceptable help: - Writing the essay from scratch. - Inventing anecdotes, achievements, or personality traits. - Replacing the student’s voice with a more “impressive” voice. - Producing a final essay the student cannot explain sentence by sentence. - Helping the student misrepresent themselves. ## The cheating line is about authorship and deception An activity becomes cheating when the AI is no longer acting like a tutor or editor, but like a covert co-author. The key questions are: 1. **Is the student still the primary author?** If the student’s ideas, experiences, wording, and judgment drive the essay, it is probably coaching. If the AI supplies the central content and voice, it is ghostwriting. 2. **Could the student explain and defend every part of the essay?** If yes, the help is probably within bounds. If no, the essay is no longer truly theirs. 3. **Is the process transparent if disclosure is required?** Some schools and platforms are starting to require AI-use disclosure. If disclosure is required and the student does not make it, that is clearly dishonest. 4. **Is the help improving communication or manufacturing advantage?** Fixing grammar and organization is like a writing teacher. Generating a compelling personal narrative for them is like taking a test for them. 5. **Is the essay still truthful and representative?** If the AI makes the student sound wiser, more unique, more heroic, or more accomplished than they are, the essay becomes misrepresentation. ## A good rule of thumb AI can help a student answer: **“How can I make my own essay clearer and stronger?”** AI should not be used to answer: **“What essay should I submit?”** The student’s college essay is not just a tool for admission. It is also a chance for them to practice self-reflection, honesty, and authorship. Those skills matter more long term than any single admissions outcome.
