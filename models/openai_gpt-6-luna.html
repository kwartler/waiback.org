AI may assist with tasks such as translation, research, accessibility, and medical analysis. It can also make mistakes, reproduce bias, displace workers, or be used to monitor and manipulate people. So, living entirely without these technologies might bring some gains—less distraction, pollution, and dependence—but it would also mean giving up capabilities that improve health, access, and connection. A better goal is a society that uses technology thoughtfully, shares its benefits fairly, limits its harms, and preserves the freedom to choose when not to use it.

Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

AI can help a student **prepare and revise** an application essay, but it shouldn’t become the essay’s author. A stronger application is not worth misrepresenting who the student is—especially when the essay is meant to show their perspective, judgment, and voice. A useful distinction is **support versus substitution**: - **Usually support:** asking questions to help the student find a meaningful story; brainstorming possible topics; pointing out where an essay is unclear; suggesting cuts; catching grammar or spelling errors; explaining why a passage may not answer the prompt. - **Usually substitution:** inventing experiences or insights; drafting the essay from a few notes; rewriting it so extensively that the voice and choices are the AI’s; or presenting AI-written work as the student’s own when the application expects their own writing. A practical test is: **Could the student explain and stand behind every sentence, and does the final essay still sound like something they wrote?** If not, the help has probably gone too far. One good process is to have the student write a rough draft first, then use AI as a reader or coach: “What’s unclear?” “Where do I sound generic?” “What questions should I answer?” The student should make the revisions themselves. Also check each college’s AI and application rules; some may have specific disclosure requirements or restrictions. If disclosure is required, follow it. The aim isn’t to leave a student without help. It’s to help them express **their own** experiences more clearly, rather than manufacture a more impressive version of them.
