Acceptable examples: - “Does this essay answer the prompt?” - “What parts feel vague or generic?” - “Where could I add more concrete detail?” - “Point out confusing sentences.” - “Suggest questions I should ask myself to make this more personal.” - “Check grammar while preserving my wording and voice.” - “Give feedback like an admissions counselor, but do not rewrite it for me.” This is similar to a tutor saying: “Your conclusion feels rushed,” or “This paragraph needs a more specific example.” ## Uses that look like cheating These are much harder to justify: - “Rewrite this to sound like an Ivy League admit.” - “Make this more sophisticated and impressive.” - “Turn these notes into a complete essay.” - “Write a compelling personal statement about overcoming adversity.” - Letting AI invent details, emotions, motivations, or insights. - Submitting AI-generated prose the student cannot explain or would not naturally write. - Violating a school’s explicit AI policy or failing to disclose use when required. Ask me questions that would help me revise it myself.” Then the student revises. AI can check grammar afterward, but the student should accept or reject changes consciously.
