If AI rewrites it, the school is no longer evaluating the student; it's evaluating a machine's polished simulation of the student. That's the core problem, regardless of outcomes: - The student typically signs (or the parent co-signs) a certification that the essay is their own work. - Many colleges now ask directly about AI use and have policies against AI-generated content. - If discovered later, consequences can include rescinded admission—well after the "better life prospects" are supposedly secured. - Ironically, heavily AI-polished essays often read as generic and sanitized, which experienced readers notice. **The "everyone gets help" argument** It's true that affluent students have long used essay coaches, and AI could theoretically level that field. But there's a difference between a coach asking probing questions and tightening a student's own sentences, and a system generating the substance of the essay. The first preserves authorship; the second replaces it. **So how do you differentiate this from cheating?** Honestly: if AI rewrites the essay and the student submits it as their own, you can't cleanly differentiate it—that *is* the textbook definition of academic dishonesty in this context.
