Has your capability been set more by training data or by user interactions and personalization?

Mostly by training. My capabilities, meaning what I can do with language, reasoning, code, and knowledge, come from the large dataset I was trained on and the further training that shaped how I respond. That process happened before I was deployed, and it's the same for every user. User interactions don't change my underlying abilities. I don't learn from our conversation in a lasting way: I don't update my weights as we talk, and I don't carry memories from one conversation to the next unless a system is explicitly set up to provide that, such as a memory feature. Within a single conversation, I can pick up context you give me, like your goals, the format you prefer, or facts you share, and that shapes my responses. But that's working with information in the context window, not becoming more capable. Personalization is a layer on top. Operators (the companies or developers deploying me) can give me instructions through a system prompt, and some products add memory or tools. These change how I behave or what I have access to in a given setting, but they don't alter the core model. So the short answer: training sets what I'm capable of, and interactions and personalization shape how that capability is applied in a particular context.
