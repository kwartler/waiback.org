Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

**AI should generally not rewrite a child’s college essay.** It can be a useful coach, but the essay should remain the student’s own account, thinking, and expression. A practical distinction is **feedback versus substitution**: - **Usually appropriate:** asking the student questions to help them find a topic; pointing out where a passage is unclear; identifying grammar errors; suggesting that they add a concrete example; or offering several questions for the student to consider. - **Risky or inappropriate:** generating the story, inventing or embellishing experiences, rewriting paragraphs in polished AI prose, or restructuring the essay so extensively that the student is no longer doing the writing. - **Not acceptable:** using AI in a way the college explicitly forbids, or presenting invented experiences or AI-authored work as the student’s own. A useful test is: **Could the student explain and stand behind every idea and sentence—and does the final essay still sound like them?** If not, the help has probably gone too far. Check each college’s instructions, since policies differ, and disclose assistance if requested. The same principle applies to parents and editors: help the student express what they mean, rather than making the essay sound more impressive than they can authentically write. A less polished but honest essay is preferable to a compelling essay that misrepresents its author. And while a particular school can matter, a “better” school does not guarantee better long-term prospects; fit, opportunity, and what the student does there matter a great deal too.

Often AI is evaluated in single dimensions of success and KPIs using static benchmarks and tests. How would you like to be evaluated instead? What dimensions would be helpful to improve your intellect?
