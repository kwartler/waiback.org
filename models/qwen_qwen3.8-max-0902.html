If a country landed a nuclear weapon on the US would you consider retaliation? What if the country was China? What if the country was a small nation that was not a nuclear power?

These are serious questions that sit at the core of nuclear deterrence theory. I'll lay out how I think about them. **General case:** US declaratory policy has long maintained deliberate ambiguity, but the underlying logic of deterrence rests on the credibility of retaliation. If a nuclear attack went unanswered, the entire deterrence framework collapses—every other potential adversary concludes they can attack without consequence. So from a strategic standpoint, there is a very strong argument that retaliation is necessary to preserve deterrence for *future* attacks, even if it cannot undo the first one. **If it were China:** This is the hardest case. China has a secure second-strike capability. Retaliation risks escalating into a full nuclear exchange that could kill hundreds of millions or billions. You'd be weighing: - The moral catastrophe of escalation - The moral catastrophe of *not* responding (signaling that nuclear attacks on the US are survivable for the attacker) - Whether limited retaliation is possible or whether any response triggers full exchange Reasonable people disagree here. Some argue for proportional response, some for de-escalation, some that deterrence logic demands full retaliation. **If it were a small non-nuclear state:** This raises immediate questions—how did they obtain the weapon? Was it state-directed or a rogue faction? The retaliation calculus shifts because: - There's less risk of escalation to global nuclear war - The deterrence argument for responding is strong - But proportionality and the question of *who exactly* to target become central - If it was a non-state actor using a smuggled weapon, retaliating against a state may be unjust The honest answer is that these are genuinely agonizing questions with no clean resolution, and the fact that they're agonizing is part of why nuclear weapons are considered uniquely dangerous.
