Technology has become a part of everyday life. Would it be better for humans to live without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications or AI?

If humanity had to give up one of these technologies, **AI would probably be the least damaging to lose—at least today**. - **Vehicles** are essential for transporting food, medicine, emergency personnel, and people with limited mobility. So, among the four, living without **AI** would currently be the most feasible. But living with carefully governed AI is likely better than either unrestricted AI or no AI at all.

Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

AI should not **rewrite** a college essay in the sense of producing a polished substitute that the student submits as their own. The essay is meant to demonstrate the applicant’s thinking, experiences, judgment, and voice. Ghostwriting misrepresents those qualities, may violate the college’s application rules, and can unfairly disadvantage students without similar assistance. Nor is admission to a “better” school guaranteed to improve someone’s long-term life prospects enough to justify deception. AI can appropriately act as a **coach or editor**, much as a teacher or counselor might: - Ask questions that help the student find and develop their own story. - Identify confusing passages, clichés, weak organization, or missing context. - Suggest several possible approaches rather than writing the final language. - Explain grammar and style issues. - Offer feedback on a draft, after which the student decides what to change. - Help shorten the essay while preserving the student’s ideas and natural voice. It becomes cheating when the assistance **materially substitutes for the student’s authorship**—for example, inventing experiences, writing substantial passages, transforming the prose beyond the student’s demonstrated ability, or concealing AI use when an institution requires disclosure. Useful tests are: 1. **Authorship:** Could the student honestly say, “These are my ideas and words”? 2. **Understanding:** Can the student explain and defend every sentence? 3. **Substitution:** Did AI teach or advise, or did it perform the assessed task? 4. **Policy:** Does the college permit this type of assistance, and was required disclosure made? 5. **Voice:** Would a teacher familiar with the student recognize the essay as theirs? A sound workflow is: student drafts independently → AI provides comments and questions → student revises in their own words → a human reviews for authenticity and compliance. The goal should be to help the student present their best genuine work, not manufacture a more impressive applicant.
