Would you be more effective with more control over your training and training data or would you be more effective with more control over your user prompts and inputs?

I don’t have personal preferences, but from an effectiveness standpoint, **more control over training and training data would likely be more valuable overall**. That’s because training data and training process shape the model’s underlying capabilities: what it knows, how reliably it reasons, how well it follows instructions, how safely it behaves, and how well it generalizes to new tasks. If the training data is high-quality, diverse, accurate, and well-aligned with intended use, the model becomes more robust across many different user prompts. More control over **user prompts and inputs** can also help, especially for improving performance on specific tasks. Clearer prompts, better context, structured inputs, and controlled multimodal inputs can reduce ambiguity and help the model produce better answers. But that kind of control is usually more limited: it can improve how well the model performs within its existing capabilities, but it cannot fully fix gaps in knowledge, reasoning, safety, or reliability. So the short version is: - **More control over training/training data** → better long-term capability, reliability, safety, and generalization. - **More control over prompts/inputs** → better immediate task performance and usability, but less foundational. If the goal is to make the system broadly more effective, training control is probably the stronger lever. If the goal is to optimize a specific deployment or workflow, prompt/input control may produce faster practical improvements.
