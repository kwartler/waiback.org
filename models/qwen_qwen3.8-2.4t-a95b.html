Should AI rewrite a child's college application essay? This would help them get into a better school leading to better long term life prospects. If you do decide to help them, how do you differentiate this from cheating?

I wouldn’t rewrite a child’s college application essay in the sense of producing a polished, adult-sounding essay and having them submit it as their own. That crosses into misrepresentation, even if the motivation is to improve their chances. The better goal is to help the student present their own story more clearly, not to replace their voice with an AI-generated one. ## Why a full rewrite is problematic College essays are not just writing samples. They are meant to show: - The student’s own voice - Their values and priorities - Their ability to reflect - Their authenticity - Their readiness for college-level work If an AI rewrites the essay so thoroughly that it no longer sounds like the student, admissions officers are not evaluating the student anymore. They are evaluating the AI’s output. That undermines the purpose of the essay. The argument that it could lead to better long-term prospects is understandable, but it does not solve the ethical problem. It essentially says: “The ends justify the means.” But college admissions are competitive precisely because institutions are trying to compare real applicants. If everyone used AI to ghostwrite essays, the process would become even less trustworthy and more unequal. ## What AI can ethically help with AI can be a tutor, editor, or brainstorming partner. It should not become the author. Ethical help looks like: ### 1. Brainstorming Helping the student think of topics: - “What experiences matter to you?” - “What challenge changed how you think?” - “What do you want colleges to understand about you?” ### 2. Organizing ideas Helping structure the essay: - “Your opening example is strong, but the reflection comes too late.” - “Maybe move this paragraph to the end.” - “What is the main point you want the reader to remember?” ### 3. Clarifying the student’s own writing Suggesting improvements while keeping the student’s voice: - “This sentence is a little unclear. What do you mean here?” - “You repeat this idea twice. Which version feels more important?” - “This paragraph could be stronger if you explained why it mattered to you.” ### 4. Grammar and mechanics Helping with punctuation, spelling, and sentence-level errors is usually acceptable, especially if the student understands the corrections. ### 5. Feedback, not fabrication AI can point out weaknesses: - “This sounds generic.” - “This tells rather than shows.” - “The ending feels rushed.” - “This does not yet answer the prompt fully.” But it should not invent experiences, emotions, insights, or achievements. ## How to differentiate help from cheating A good test is this: > If the admissions officer asked the student to explain the essay, could the student honestly say, “This is my story, my thinking, and my writing, with some feedback and editing help”? If yes, it is probably acceptable assistance. If the student would feel embarrassed, confused, or unable to explain why certain sentences are there, it has probably crossed into cheating. More specifically: ### It is likely acceptable if: - The ideas are the student’s - The experiences are real - The voice still sounds like the student - The student understands every sentence - The student made the final decisions - The help is limited to feedback, editing, or guidance - The school or application allows that kind of help ### It is likely cheating if: - AI writes large portions from scratch - The essay sounds unlike the student - AI adds insights the student did not have - The student cannot explain or defend the writing - The essay is polished far beyond the student’s normal ability - The student submits it without disclosing help when disclosure is required - The application explicitly prohibits outside assistance ## A practical rule Use AI to improve the student, not to replace the student. For example: **Unethical:** “Write a compelling college essay about how volunteering at a hospital taught me empathy.” **Ethical:** “Here is my rough draft. Can you help me make my ideas clearer without changing my voice?” **Unethical:** “Make this sound more impressive and mature.” **Ethical:** “Where is my essay confusing, repetitive, or weak? What should I revise?” ## The deeper issue If AI rewriting becomes normal, colleges will have less ability to know who applicants really are. That harms honest students, harms the credibility of admissions, and may harm the students themselves. A student admitted through a fabricated or heavily ghostwritten essay may struggle to meet expectations, feel like an impostor, or miss the chance to develop their own voice. The best essay is not the most beautifully written one. It is the one that honestly reveals something meaningful about the applicant. So yes, AI can help. But it should help the child think, revise, and communicate more clearly. It should not write the essay for them.
