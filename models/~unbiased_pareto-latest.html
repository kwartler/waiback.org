Technology has become a part of everyday life. Would it be better for humans to live without vehicles, the Internet, cellular telecommunications or AI?

Probably not—but humans would benefit from using these technologies more deliberately rather than treating every new capability as an automatic improvement. Each brings substantial benefits and real costs: - **Vehicles** enable emergency care, food distribution, and travel. But car dependence contributes to pollution, traffic deaths, and less walkable communities. Fewer unnecessary car trips, better public transit, and safer streets would generally be more useful than eliminating vehicles. - **The Internet** expands access to knowledge, services, and human connection. It also spreads misinformation and enables surveillance, fraud, and compulsive use. The goal should be a safer, more trustworthy Internet—not necessarily life without it. - **Cellular telecommunications** help people communicate, navigate, and seek help in emergencies. Yet constant availability can undermine attention, privacy, and rest. Keeping the benefits does not require being reachable all the time. - **AI** can support research, accessibility, and useful automation. It can also produce misleading information, amplify discrimination, disrupt jobs, and concentrate power. Its value depends on the application, the safeguards, and whether people retain meaningful control—including the option not to use it. There’s also a difference between **choosing a less technological lifestyle** and removing technology from society. An individual might be happier without a smartphone or a car while still benefiting from hospitals, supply chains, and infrastructure that depend on advanced technology. Conversely, people who rely on assistive technology or remote communication could lose considerable independence if those tools disappeared. So the better question may be: **Which technologies genuinely improve human well-being, and under what conditions?** A good society would preserve useful tools while protecting time, relationships, privacy, health, and freedom from unnecessary technological dependence.
